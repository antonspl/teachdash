# TeachDash product brief

TeachDash supports private tutors who teach individual students across disciplines. A tutor holds lessons on the platform, and the student returns between lessons to use the materials and stay in touch with their tutor.

## People and their week

| Person | What brings them back | Device and context |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tutor | Teaches one student at a time. Between lessons they may reply to a message or add material for the student. | A desktop or laptop for teaching. Phone use is occasional, mainly for those between-lesson tasks. |
| Student | Returns between lessons to find material from their tutor or chat with them. Students can be any age, though most are adults. | A desktop or laptop is typical. A phone serves the same two purposes. |
| Administrator | Manages teacher accounts, including account access. | Frequency and device use are open. |

The planning figure in the project constitution is about 1,000 teachers. For design planning, assume about 10 active students per tutor on average, often more. The expected administrator count is open. Chat describes an intended workflow in this brief.

## What this means for the design

- Use terms and examples that work across subjects. A language course can have a target language. Other subjects use the same course and lesson structure.
- Judge the desktop experience against a tutor conducting a lesson. Judge the student experience on both desktop and phone against two concrete tasks: finding the relevant material and reaching the tutor.
- Use clear, friendly, informal copy in sentence case. Use language that suits adults while remaining accessible to younger learners.
- Design for English and Russian UI text, including Cyrillic, and meet WCAG 2.2 AA.

The project constraints come from sections 8 and 9 of `openspec/constitution.md` and from `.agents/design/project-rules.md`. The current interface can be checked against the specs in `openspec/specs/web-auth/`, `openspec/specs/web-profile/` and `openspec/specs/web-admin-teachers/`.
